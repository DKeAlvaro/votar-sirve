# Does voting change anything for young people in Spain?

Pre-registration. Written 2026-11-24, **before any data for these charts was looked at**.
Predictions are drafted from Álvaro's stated thesis. A prediction may only be edited before
the data for that chart is opened; after that, git history is the referee.

## Thesis (Álvaro, 23)

Voting PP vs PSOE does not measurably change what matters to a young person: becoming
independent (salary, housing) and starting a business (autónomo fees, red tape, social
acceptance). The problems are structural, take decades, and are partly cultural (low ambition,
"best aspiration is becoming a funcionario"). The Netherlands works much better.

## Impartiality rules

1. Every series starts at the first year with data, never a hand-picked year.
2. All money in 2025 euros with the same deflator (INE CPI). Robustness: young-renter basket.
3. Y axis starts at 0 for levels.
4. Always a comparison: another country, age group or region. A lone line proves nothing.
5. Background shaded by government; shocks marked (euro 2002, crisis 2008, COVID 2020).
6. Primary sources only (INE, Eurostat, Seguridad Social, AEAT, BdE, Catastro, ministries,
   CBS, OECD, survey microdata or official crosstabs). No newspaper or think-tank numbers.
7. Every chart is published, including the ones that contradict the thesis.

Governments: UCD 1977-82 · PSOE 1982-96 · PP 1996-2004 · PSOE 2004-11 · PP 2011-18 · PSOE 2018-.

## Charts, predictions, and what refutes them

### Is it harder to become independent?

**1. At what age do we leave home?** % of 25-34 living with parents, 1981-2025, ES vs NL vs EU.
- Prediction: rises roughly continuously in ES since the 1980s, whoever governs; NL low and flat.
- Refuted if: the rise is concentrated in one shock (e.g. post-2008) or reverses in some period.

**2. How many years of salary does a flat cost?** Price of 80 m² / annual salary of a 25-34
year old, 1987-2025. Twin: % of a young salary taken by an average new rental.
- Prediction: clearly higher now than in the late 80s/90s on both measures.
- Refuted if: similar or lower than then.

**3. Young renter's prices vs young salary.** Two lines, 2008 = 100: a basket weighted like a
young renter's spending (rent at new-contract prices) vs salary of 25-34.
- Prediction: the price line ends above the salary line.
- Refuted if: young salaries kept pace or beat it.

**4. Are enough homes being built?** Bars: homes completed per year vs new households per
year, 1990-2025.
- Prediction: completions below household formation since ~2012 under every government.
- Refuted if: some government closed the gap.

### Does who governs change anything?

**5. The governments panel.** Six small charts, background by government, each with a control
line (Portugal or EU average): youth unemployment; job stability measured in a way that a
contract relabel cannot fake (days contributed per year per young worker, or average tenure of
25-34 in their current job; NOT the temporary-contract rate); real salary of 25-34; years of
young salary per flat; % of 25-34 living with parents; real minimum autónomo fee.
Because 4 years is short, the shading is also tested shifted by +2 and +4 years (policy lag).
- Reading rule: for each metric take ES minus control and compare its slope inside each
  government period with the previous period.
- Prediction: no consistent party pattern beyond the control, at any lag.
- Refuted if: at least 3 of the 6 metrics show slope changes at changes of government with a
  consistent sign by party (one party's periods systematically better), at some lag.

**6. Before and after each law.** X axis = months from the law (−36 to +36), affected group vs
unaffected group:
- 2021 labour reform: days contributed / tenure of under-30s vs over-45s (and ES vs PT).
- 2019 minimum-wage jump (+22%): youth employment in provinces where the SMI bites hard (low
  median wage) vs where it barely bites.
- 2023 autónomo fee reform: new autónomo registrations under 30 vs 30+.
- Prediction: affected and control lines stay parallel after month 0.
- Refuted if: the post-law average gap falls outside the range the gap had in the 36 pre-law months.

**7. Town vs town (close municipal elections).** X = PP winning margin in municipal elections
(−10 to +10 points), Y = homes built in the next 4 years per 1,000 inhabitants (also IBI rate,
registered residents aged 20-34). Data: Ministry of Interior results, Catastro year of
construction, Padrón. All administrative, no surveys.
- Prediction: no jump at 0.
- Refuted if: the jump at 0 has a 95% CI excluding zero.

### Culture or costs?

**8. Is starting a business frowned upon?** % saying entrepreneurship is a good career choice,
% for whom fear of failure would stop them, % preferring self-employment, ES vs NL vs EU, since 2005.
- Prediction: ES clearly worse than NL (more than 5 points) on attitudes.
- Refuted if: within 5 points, i.e. the gap is costs, not mindset.

**9. What does being autónomo cost?** Line: X = annual net income 0-60,000 €, Y = what you pay
(social contributions + income tax), ES vs NL, 2026 rules. Plus real minimum autónomo fee 1980-2026.
- Prediction: ES charges you at 0 € income, NL does not; ES more expensive at low incomes.
- Refuted if: ES is cheaper over most of the range.

**10. Is wanting to be a funcionario rational?** Salary and job stability, public vs private,
by education level; public employment as % of total in ES, NL, Nordics, OECD average.
- Prediction: ES has more public employment than NL/OECD and the public premium is small
  (so the preference is cultural).
- Refuted if: ES public employment ≤ OECD average, or the public job pays more and is safer
  for mid-education workers (so the preference is a rational calculation).

### Spain vs the Netherlands

**11. Health: what you pay vs what you get.** Scatter of EU countries, X = health spending per
person (PPP), Y = life expectancy; second scatter with Y = % with unmet medical needs due to
waiting lists. ES and NL highlighted. Also treatable mortality.
- Prediction: NL beats ES on waiting-list unmet needs and treatable mortality.
- Refuted if: ES is equal or better on those at lower cost.

**12. Where do your contributions go?** Stacked bar for a 30,000 € gross salary: share paying
current pensions, share to your own pension fund, share income tax. ES vs NL.
- Prediction: a young ES worker sends a larger share of salary to current pensioners.
- Refuted if: similar shares.

**13. Who votes?** Votes cast by age band (population × turnout), 1982 vs 2023; next to it,
public spending per person 65+ vs per person under 30 over time.
- Prediction: the 65+ share of votes and the spending gap both widened, under PP and PSOE alike.
- Refuted if: the gap did not widen, or it differs by party.

### Qualitative: hope, effort, ambition (survey microdata, only identically worded questions)

**A. Do young people expect things to get better?** % of 18-29 expecting the next 12 months to
be better, vs 50+, 1985-2025, ES vs NL.
- Prediction: young Spaniards are less optimistic now than in the 80s, and less than young Dutch.
- Refuted if: not lower now, or not lower than NL.

**B. Do they believe effort pays off?** % of young who say hard work brings a better life (vs
luck and connections), 1981-~2018, ES vs NL.
- Prediction: lower in ES than NL, and falling in ES.
- Refuted if: similar to NL or not falling.

**C. Where do they want to work?** Public job / private company / own business, young people
1984 vs 2024.
- Prediction: more want a public job now than in 1984.
- Refuted if: flat or falling.
