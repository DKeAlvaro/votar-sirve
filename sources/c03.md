# Sources for chart c03 (young renter's prices vs young salary)

Downloaded 2026-11-24/25. Scripts: `scripts/c03_epf_weights.py`, `scripts/c03_cpi_groups.py`. Raw in `data/raw/c03/`. Salary side of c03 uses `data/clean/c02_salaries.csv`; new-contract rent side uses `data/clean/c02_rents.csv` (see `sources/c02.md`).

## Weights -> `c03_weights.csv`
- **INE_EPF_2008, INE_EPF_2013, INE_EPF_2019, INE_EPF_2025** - INE Encuesta de Presupuestos Familiares, microdata files (hogar + gastos) in `data/raw/c03/epf/` (epf_2008.zip, epf_2013.zip, datos_2019.zip, datos_2025.zip; INE https://www.ine.es/dyngs/INEbase/es/operacion.htm?c=Estadistica_C&cid=1254736176806). Locally present: 2006-2015, 2019, 2025 (2016-18 and 2020-24 folders are empty). Only the four required years were processed.
- Method: households whose reference person (sustentador principal, `EDADSP`) is under 35 AND tenure `REGTEN` in (3 rent, 4 reduced rent). Share = sum(GASTO in ECOICOP group) / sum(GASTO all groups) across households, in % (GASTO is already grossed up to the population by INE, so the sum is population-weighted). Group = first 2 digits of `CODIGO`. Rent = codes starting 0411 (actual rent of main dwelling), imputed rent = 0421. Comparison groups in the file: `young_renter` (main), `young_all` (<35, any tenure), `all_renter`, `all_households`. `n_households_sample` = unweighted sample size (young_renter: 640 in 2008, 692 in 2013, 582 in 2019, 559 in 2025: modest, so shares carry sampling error of roughly +-1 pt for large groups).
- Check: total spending grossed up = 541 bn EUR (2008), 494 (2013), 567 (2019), 688 (2025).
- Breaks: 2008-2019 use ECOICOP v1 (12 groups: 01 food, 02 alcohol/tobacco, 03 clothing, 04 housing/utilities, 05 furnishings, 06 health, 07 transport, 08 communications, 09 leisure/culture, 10 education, 11 restaurants/hotels, 12 other). 2025 uses COICOP 2018 (13 groups: 08 = information and communication, 09 recreation, 12 = insurance and financial services, 13 = personal care/social protection/misc; 02 = tobacco/alcohol/narcotics but narrower), so group-level shares for 02, 08, 09, 12, 13 are not comparable with earlier years; use groups 01, 04 (and rent), 07 for trend. Group 04 in the survey includes imputed rent of owners (zero for renters except reduced/free). The EPF changed the reference population (Census 2011 revision from 2008 onwards; 2019+ also new sample design).
- GASTO scaling: the 2019 parquet carries two implied decimals (divided by 100 in the script); irrelevant for shares.
- Result: young renter actual-rent share 20.3% (2008), 23.6% (2013), 24.4% (2019), 25.2% (2025); housing group 04 25.4, 29.9, 29.9, 33.0.

## CPI by group -> `c03_cpi_groups.csv`
- **INE_24080** - INE IPC, table 24080 "Indices nacionales de grupos ECOICOP" (API series IPC251859 ... IPC251847, 12 groups, base 2021), monthly 2002-01..2025-12 -> annual averages 2002-2025. Group codes `ecoicop1_01..12` (no general index in this file; use `common_cpi_es.csv`).
- **INE_76125** - INE IPC, table 76125 "Indices nacionales: general y de grupos ECOICOP ver.2" (COICOP 2018, 13 groups plus general `coicop2018_00`, base 2025), 2002-2025 annual averages (back-cast by INE). Use this one to match the 2025 EPF weights; use ecoicop1 for 2008-2019 weights. 2026 not included (incomplete year).
- Raw: `ipc_24080_all.json`, `ipc_76125.json`. Annual average only when all 12 months exist.
- Rent subclass (longer, back to 1976) is in `c02_rents.csv` (`cpi_rent_annual_avg`).
- Note: INE CPI rent measures rent paid by all tenants (stock); the c03 "rent at new-contract prices" variant should use INCASOL (Catalonia 2005-), IPVA new-contract index (2021-24) and SERPAVI (stock, 2011-24) from `c02_rents.csv`.
