# Chart 13 - Who votes? (votes by age band; spending per person 65+ vs under 30)

Format of all clean files: `date,geo,group,variable,value,unit,source_id`.

## eurostat_demo_pjan -> data/clean/c13_population_single_age.csv, c13_population_age_bands.csv
- Eurostat `demo_pjan` "Population on 1 January by age and sex", sex=T, geo ES and NL. Raw `data/raw/c13/demo_pjan.json`
  (API https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/demo_pjan?format=JSON&lang=en&geo=ES&geo=NL&sex=T, 2026-11-24).
  Script `scripts/c13_eurostat.py`.
- `c13_population_single_age.csv`: group `Y0`..`Y100` (Y100 = 100 and over), variable `population`, from 1971, persons on 1 January.
- `c13_population_age_bands.csv`: group `total`, `0-17`, `18-29`, `30-44`, `45-64`, `65+`, `0-29`, `20-34`, `18+`; sums of single ages, only where
  every single age exists. This is residents, not electors: foreigners (no vote in general elections), people turning 18 after the election, and the
  CERA electorate abroad are not netted out. Use Interior electors for calibration.

## eurostat_gov_10a_exp -> data/clean/c13_cofog.csv
- Eurostat `gov_10a_exp` "General government expenditure by function (COFOG)", sector S13, na_item TE, geo ES and NL, functions TOTAL, GF07 health,
  GF09 education, GF10 social protection and GF1001 sickness/disability, GF1002 old age, GF1003 survivors, GF1004 family/children,
  GF1005 unemployment, GF1006 housing, GF1007 social exclusion n.e.c., GF1008 R&D social protection, GF1009 n.e.c. Raw `data/raw/c13/gov_10a_exp.json`;
  from 1995. Script `scripts/c13_eurostat.py`.
- Variables: `cofog_expenditure_eur_m` (EUR million, nominal), `cofog_expenditure_nac_m` (national currency, EUR for both), `cofog_expenditure_pct_gdp`,
  `cofog_expenditure_pct_total_exp`. Nominal figures need central deflation (ES CPI in `common_cpi_es.csv`; NL needs its own CPI, or compare in % of GDP).
- Caveats: COFOG is not split by age. Old age (GF1002) proxies spending on 65+, education (GF09) + family (GF1004) proxy under-30s; health (GF07)
  cannot be split by age. Series start 1995, so 1982-94 are missing (would need IGAE / Ministerio de Hacienda tables). Revisions as published.

## cis_<study> -> data/clean/c13_turnout_age.csv  (script `scripts/c13_turnout_age.py`)
- Publisher: CIS (Centro de Investigaciones Sociologicas), post-electoral studies, free microdata, no registration. Fetched by
  `scripts/c13_cis_download.py` (`https://www.cis.es/documents/20117/<fileid>/MD<study>.zip`) into `data/raw/c13/cis/<study>/x/` (SPSS `.sav`, or
  fixed-width `DA<study>` + SPSS syntax `ES<study>`). About 93 MB; do not re-download. All 14 general elections 1982-2023 are covered, nothing missing.
- Election / study / participation variable: 1982 #1327 P1; 1986 #1542 P10; 1989 #1842 P10; 1993 #2061 P10; 1996 #2210 P8; 2000 #2384 P32;
  2004 #2559 P7 (age = P26 in cols 138-139: the column numbers in codigo2559.pdf are off by 9, verified from the age distribution); 2008 #2757 p32;
  2011 #2920 p53; 2015 #3126 P29; 2016 #3145 P29; 2019-04 #3248 P23 (vote recall); 2019-11 #3269 B22 (vote recall); 2023 #3420 PARTICIPACIONG1.
  Fixed-width studies (2061, 2384, 2559, 3126) are parsed from the DATA LIST syntax in `ES<study>`.
- Definition: voted = "went and voted" (2023 also "voted by post"); not voted = the other valid answers ("went but could not vote", "could not vote",
  "preferred not to vote", "usually votes but not this time"); excluded = no age / no right to vote, does not remember, N.C. For both 2019 studies the
  participation question is only asked of abstainers, so participation comes from vote recall: any party, blank or null vote = voted, code 97 ("No voto" /
  "Abstencion") = not voted; recall N.C. (13-14% of the sample) is excluded. Ages 18-97 only (99 = N.C. dropped).
- Variables: `self_reported_turnout_pct_unweighted` (all studies), `self_reported_turnout_pct_weighted` (PESO; only where the study has a weight:
  1986, 1989, 1996, 2004, 2008, 2011, 2015, 2019-11, 2023; none for 1982, 1993, 2000, 2016, 2019-04), `n_valid` (respondents with valid vote answer and age).
  Groups: 18-24, 25-29, 30-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55-64, 65-74, 75+, and aggregates 18-29, 30-44, 45-64, 65+, 18+. Unit "% of valid answers".
  Date = YYYY; the two 2019 elections are 2019-04 and 2019-11.
- Calibration: `official_turnout_pct` (group `all`) sits next to the `18+` self-reported rows. Self-reported turnout overshoots the official figure by
  4-20 points in every study (1982: 88.5 vs 80.0; 2023: 89.0 vs 70.4): the usual over-reporting plus non-response of abstainers, and in 2011 / 2015 a
  panel re-interviewing pre-electoral respondents. Use the age profile, rescaled so the weighted total equals the official total (e.g. multiply or logit-shift
  band turnout), not the raw levels. Over-reporting may differ by age, which cannot be corrected from these files.
- Caveats: samples per band are about 100-500 in the old studies (see `n_valid`); 2023 (10,090 valid) is the largest. Voting age was 18 throughout.

## interior_Elecciones-Congreso -> data/clean/c13_turnout_official.csv  (script `scripts/c13_turnout_official.py`)
- Publisher: Ministerio del Interior open data "Elecciones Congreso" (XLSX, CC BY 4.0, last updated 2024-01-01):
  https://descargas.interior.gob.es/datasets/resultados_electorales/Elecciones-Congreso.xlsx, listed at
  https://infoelectoral.interior.gob.es/es/elecciones-celebradas/datos-abiertos/. Downloaded 2026-11-25 into `data/raw/c13/interior/`.
  The infoelectoral pages sit behind a Cloudflare challenge for curl; the `descargas` host does not.
- Variables, group `all`, 1977-2023 (16 elections, date YYYY, 2019-04 and 2019-11): `official_turnout_pct` = (Votantes - Votantes CERA) / Electores CER
  (residents only, the headline figure: 1982 79.97, 2019-04 75.75, 2019-11 69.86, 2023 70.41) and `official_turnout_incl_cera_pct` = Votantes / Electores
  (includes the CERA electorate abroad, whose turnout is about 30%: 2023 66.59). Sums over the 52 province columns. Before 1986 there is no CERA, so both
  are equal. `c13_turnout_age.csv` copies these rows for 1982 onward.
